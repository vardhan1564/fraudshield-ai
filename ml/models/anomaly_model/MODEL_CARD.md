## Purpose
Flags behaviour unusual for the customer (new device, SIM swap, odd velocity or typing) without needing fraud labels. Output `anomaly_score` (0-1) feeds `meta_model`; it targets account takeover and card testing.

## Training data
Simulated UPI data, **legit payments only** from the training period (time split 70/15/15). Training data hash `5d3b5b91e161f344`.

## Features
14 behaviour, device and velocity features: `txn_count_5m`, `txn_count_1h`, `txn_count_24h`, `amount_sum_1h`, `amount_sum_24h`, `failed_count_1h`, `device_is_new`, `device_rooted`, `ip_distance_km`, `is_vpn`, `sim_changed_7d`, `typing_speed_z`, `session_len_s`, `login_to_pay_s`.
Counts and amounts are log1p-scaled, then standardised on train-legit rows.

## Thresholds
- Kept: **autoencoder** (better validation recall than the alternative); 50 epochs, hidden [8, 4].
- Raw score min-max scaled on validation to 0-1; flag threshold 0.00141 = 1% FPR on validation.

## Limitations
- Trained on simulated UPI data: scam signatures are cleaner than real life, so simulated scores overstate real performance (the IEEE-CIS benchmark is the realistic reference).
- Low recall on digital arrest / mule rings by design: the victim uses their own device and habits. Those are the social and graph models' job.

## Fairness results
Not measured on its own: fairness is checked on the final risk_score (`ml/reports/eval_<date>.md`), the only score that drives decisions.
